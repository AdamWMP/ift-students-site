## What Irish clients want #1: Consistency, not perfection

Most Irish clients aren’t aiming for a bodybuilding lifestyle.

They want to be consistent while still having:
- work
- kids
- social life
- weekends
- travel
- stress

They don’t want a plan that collapses the moment they miss one session.

They want a coach who helps them stay on track even when life gets messy.

This is why modern coaching in Ireland has shifted toward:
- minimum standards
- flexible routines
- habit-based targets
- realistic nutrition systems

A coach who can say:
“Here’s the plan for a good week… and here’s the plan for a bad week.”
…will keep clients far longer than someone who demands perfection.

How to deliver this:
- create a “minimum viable week” plan (2 sessions + steps + protein)
- give clients a weekend strategy
- teach them how to get back on track without guilt

## What Irish clients want #2: Clear structure and a simple plan

Irish clients are overwhelmed by fitness information.

They’ve seen:
- low carb
- keto
- fasting
- carnivore
- detox
- “train like this celebrity”
- “burn fat fast”
- “10k steps or you’re failing”

They don’t need more information.

They need clarity.

Your job as a coach in Ireland is to simplify the process.

Clients want:
- a clear training plan
- a clear nutrition target
- a clear accountability rhythm
- clear progress measures

If your coaching feels confusing, clients won’t stick with it.

How to deliver this:
- give them 3 training days and make it repeatable
- set 2 nutrition targets (protein + calories or portions)
- set one habit target (steps or sleep)
- track progress monthly, not daily obsession

## What Irish clients want #3: Accountability and coaching support (not just workouts)

This is the biggest change in the Irish market.

People don’t want a PT who only shows up when they’re paid per hour.
They want a coach who:
- checks in
- guides
- supports
- adjusts
- keeps them accountable

The “session-only” model is fading because it doesn’t solve the real problem:
Most clients struggle between sessions.

That’s why hybrid coaching is booming in Ireland.

Hybrid coaching includes:
- in-person sessions
- online programme access
- weekly check-ins
- nutrition support
- habit coaching

It gives clients the feeling:
“I’m not doing this alone.”

How to deliver this:
- weekly check-in form
- consistent response from you
- monthly progress review
- simple targets between sessions

## What Irish clients want #4: Flexibility around food (especially weekends)

Ireland is a social country.

Clients are going to:
- dinners
- nights out
- family events
- weekends away
- celebrations

And they’re not going to stop living just to get lean.

They want a plan that works with real life.

What clients are sick of:
- “no alcohol”
- “no carbs”
- “no treats”
- “you ruined the week”
- guilt-based coaching

The best Irish coaches teach clients:
- how to manage weekends
- how to recover on Monday without punishment
- how to hit protein consistently
- how to stay in control without restriction

How to deliver this:
- teach “weekend budget” strategies
- create a Monday/Tuesday reset routine
- focus on weekly averages, not daily perfection

## What Irish clients want #5: A coach who communicates like a human

This is cultural.

Irish people respond to coaches who are:
- relatable
- supportive
- honest
- a bit of craic
- real

They don’t respond well to robotic coaching.

They also don’t respond well to aggressive, influencer-style motivation.

They want:
- encouragement
- accountability
- humour when appropriate
- clear feedback
- no judgement

How to deliver this:
- use voice notes occasionally
- celebrate small wins
- call out patterns with kindness
- keep language simple and direct

## What Irish clients want #6: Progress tracking that feels motivating (not obsessive)

Many clients in Ireland have a history of dieting.

They don’t want:
- daily weigh-ins that trigger anxiety
- obsessive macro counting if it’s not needed
- a coaching plan that feels restrictive

They want progress tracking that feels:
- simple
- motivating
- evidence-based
- realistic

How to deliver this:
- use weekly weight trends (if appropriate)
- track strength progress in key lifts
- track photos monthly
- track energy, confidence, consistency
- celebrate habit wins, not just scale drops

## What Irish clients want #7: Coaching that makes them feel safe and confident

This matters more than most trainers realise.

A lot of Irish clients feel:
- intimidated in gyms
- embarrassed about their fitness level
- nervous about judgement
- confused about equipment

They don’t want a coach who makes them feel stupid.

They want a coach who makes them feel capable.

The best coaches build confidence through:
- simple coaching language
- regressions
- progressions
- patience
- education

This is why “beginner-friendly coaching” is one of the strongest niches in Ireland right now.

## What Irish clients want #8: Education that empowers them

Clients in Ireland don’t want to be dependent on a coach forever.

They want to learn:
- what works
- how to train correctly
- how to eat better
- how to manage stress
- how to build habits

Education builds retention because clients feel in control.

How to deliver education:
- small lessons weekly
- “why we’re doing this” explanations
- simple nutrition understanding
- clear training principles

## What Irish clients are sick of in 2026

Now let’s flip it.

If you want more retention and more referrals, avoid these.

1) Extreme dieting
2) Overcomplication
3) One-size-fits-all programmes
4) No check-ins / no accountability
5) “Motivation-only” coaching
6) Shame and guilt tactics
7) Random workouts with no progression
8) Coaches who aren’t qualified or credible

Irish clients are smarter than ever.
They can feel professionalism.
And they can feel chaos.

These upgrades turn your coaching into a premium experience.

And premium experiences retain clients longer.
