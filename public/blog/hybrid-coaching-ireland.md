## Why hybrid coaching is booming in Ireland

## The biggest mistake trainers make is assuming hybrid coaching is just a “trend.”

It’s not.

It’s a response to how Irish people live now.

Here’s what’s changed in Ireland over the last few years:
1) Hybrid work and remote work are normal
Clients aren’t locked into 9–5 office routines the way they were before. That means training availability is wider, but schedules are less predictable.

2) People want flexibility without losing support
Irish clients want accountability. They just don’t want their fitness life to collapse when work gets hectic, travel happens, or the kids get sick.

3) Consumers are more educated
Irish gym-goers now understand basics like protein, steps, progressive overload, and calorie awareness. They’re not looking for a trainer to “tell them what to do” once a week. They want a coach who builds a full system around them.

4) People are paying more — but expecting more value
In Ireland, people will happily pay for coaching when it feels worth it. What they’re not paying for anymore is “an hour of exercise” with no plan outside that hour.

Hybrid coaching fits all of this perfectly.

## What hybrid coaching really is (and what it is NOT)

Let’s simplify this.

Hybrid coaching is NOT:
- “online coaching plus a random PT session whenever they feel like it”
- “a gym programme PDF”
- “training sessions with a WhatsApp message once a month”

Hybrid coaching IS:
- structured training plans delivered professionally
- a predictable weekly coaching rhythm
- clear targets for training, nutrition, and habits
- consistent communication and accountability
- measurable progress tracking
- the feeling that the client has support even when you’re not physically beside them

## The best hybrid model makes the client think:
“I’m never on my own. I always know what to do next.”

That’s what drives retention.
That’s what drives results.
And that’s what drives monthly recurring revenue.

## How to transition from in-person PT to hybrid (without losing clients)

This is where trainers get stuck.

They think:
“My clients won’t pay monthly.”

They will — if you frame it correctly.

Here’s the transition plan:

Step 1: Add value before you increase price
Before changing your pricing model, upgrade your current service:
- start weekly check-ins
- create simple nutrition targets
- deliver training plans in an app
- start tracking progress more clearly

Clients will feel the difference quickly.

Step 2: Introduce hybrid as the “best results” pathway
Instead of saying “I’m increasing my prices,” say:

“I’m introducing a new coaching model that will help you get better results because it gives you support between sessions.”

Then explain:
- structure
- accountability
- progression
- nutrition support
- habit coaching

Step 3: Offer a simple upgrade path
Let clients choose:
- PT only (basic)
- Hybrid coaching (best results)

You’ll be surprised how many choose hybrid.

Step 4: Package it monthly
Monthly billing is what creates business stability.
It also increases compliance because clients treat it as a commitment.

## The hybrid coaching systems you NEED (so you don’t burn out)

Hybrid coaching only scales if you have systems.

Here’s your starter system:

1) A coaching app
Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit, MyPTHub are all common in Ireland.

2) A weekly check-in form
Use Google Forms with questions like:
- How many sessions did you complete?
- Average steps?
- Nutrition score (1–10)?
- Biggest win?
- Biggest challenge?
- What do we adjust?

3) A response template
You don’t want to write a fresh essay every week.
You want a structured response that takes 3–8 minutes per client.

4) A progress tracking system
- weight trend
- measurements
- photos
- strength lifts
- step average

5) A client community (optional but powerful)
WhatsApp groups, small group sessions, or monthly meet-ups.

Ireland is community-driven.
Community increases retention.
