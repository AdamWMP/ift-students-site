## The 5 adherence levers every Irish coach needs

Lever 1: Minimum standards
This is the single biggest game-changer.

Instead of telling clients “do everything,” set minimum standards:
- 2 training sessions per week
- 7k–10k steps average
- protein target daily
- 2 litres of water

A client can miss the perfect plan but still hit minimum standards.

That prevents “all-or-nothing” collapse.

Lever 2: Habit stacking
Attach new habits to existing routines.

Example:
- protein with breakfast
- 10 minute walk after lunch
- gym bag packed after dinner

Small habits beat motivation.

Lever 3: Weekly check-in rhythm
Weekly check-ins are the retention engine.

A simple check-in form + coach response creates:
- accountability
- progress awareness
- adjustment systems

Irish clients stay longer when they feel supported.

Lever 4: Identity coaching
The best coaches help clients become:
- “a person who trains”
- “a person who eats protein”
- “a person who moves daily”

It’s identity, not motivation, that creates adherence.

Lever 5: Environment design
If the environment is chaotic, adherence is hard.

Teach clients to:
- stock protein options
- plan lunches
- reduce trigger foods at home
- set reminders for steps

## The “bad week protocol” (Ireland-proof consistency)

This is crucial.

Most clients quit after a bad week.

Create a protocol:
- 2 short workouts (20 minutes)
- daily steps target
- simple protein rule
- sleep focus

Tell clients:
“A bad week isn’t failure. It’s part of the plan.”

This single mindset shift reduces dropouts massively.

Accountability systems that work in Ireland

System 1: Weekly check-in form
System 2: Habit score (1–10)
System 3: Monthly progress review
System 4: Streak tracking (training consistency)
System 5: Community (semi-private groups)

Ireland is community-driven.
Community increases adherence.

## How to improve adherence without becoming strict

Irish clients hate being lectured.

Use coaching language like:
- “let’s make this easier”
- “what’s the smallest version you can do?”
- “how do we reduce friction?”
- “what’s realistic this week?”

Supportive accountability beats aggressive pressure.
